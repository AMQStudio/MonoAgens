# R-02 social_cost
*File: research/findings/R-02-social-cost.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
The floor belongs to someone else when the trigger selected another next speaker, or when a third party has already responded. Closeness to the sender lowers the cost.

social_cost(e) = max(sel_other(e), answered(e)) · (1 − λ_c · closeness(sender(e)))
- sel_other = 1 if e's addressee set is non-empty and excludes the soul. With R-29's graded address: clamp(max_{l≠soul} deg(l) − deg(soul), 0, 1).
- answered = 1 if an event in place(e) by an actor other than the sender and the soul falls in (t(e), now]. This reuses patience's test.
- closeness(p) = σ(B_p) over exchanges with p (R-04); 0 if there are none. λ_c ∈ [0, 1].

## Parameters
| Name | Meaning | Start value | Range | Source or "tune in Lab" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| λ_c | closeness discount | 0.5 | 0–1 | tune in Lab |

## Neutral value
w_sc = 0.

## Claim
Without it, the soul more often replies after another person was selected or has already answered (measured per 100 such events).

## How the Lab would test it
Group scenarios with explicit selections and already-answered questions. Compare floor violations against missed help, with w_sc on and off, and λ_c at 0 vs. 0.5.

## Established / inferred / open
Established: a selected speaker has the right and obligation to speak (Sacks, Schegloff & Jefferson 1974). Inferred: the formula. Open: whether closeness should lower the cost at all (no source found), and whether social_cost is redundant with patience. The rule is chat-centred until R-29 is done.

## Sources
Sacks, Schegloff & Jefferson (1974), *Language* 50(4) [PR].

# R-04 Familiarity and closeness
*File: research/findings/R-04-familiarity-closeness.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
**What counts as an interaction.**
- *Presence* in place P (an event in P whose audience contains the soul, or the soul's own act in P) feeds place familiarity.
- An *exchange* with p feeds closeness: p's event addressed to the soul (or with address degree ≥ 0.5), the soul's act addressed to p, or a reply-linked pair.
- Co-presence alone is not an exchange, so a 500-member channel does not make everyone close.

**Combining place and people.** In ACT-R style, activation is divided across sources: B_ctx = (B_P + Σ_{p∈Present} B_p)/(1 + |Present|). familiarity = σ(B_ctx), or 0 with no history.

**Base-level formula.** Use Petrov's hybrid, because d is a knob:
B ≈ ln[Σ_{j=1}^{k} t_j^(−d) + (n−k)(t_n^(1−d) − t_k^(1−d))/((1−d)(t_n − t_k))]
where t_j are the ages in hours of the k most recent uses, t_n is the age of the first use, and n is the use count. Floor t at one minute.

## Parameters
| Name | Meaning | Start value | Range | Source or "tune in Lab" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k | exact recent uses | 1 | 1–3 | Petrov 2006; tune in Lab |
| exchange threshold | min address degree | 0.5 | 0.3–0.8 | tune in Lab |

## Neutral value
r = 0; λ_c = 0.

## Claim
Without familiarity, false-speak early in a new place is no lower than in a familiar one.

## How the Lab would test it
The soul joins a group, returns after a week, and returns again after 90 days away. Run with r on and off, and check that familiarity decays during the absence.

## Established / inferred / open
Established: power-law base level (Anderson & Schooler 1991); the hybrid approximation (Petrov 2006); non-monotonicity of the standard form (Fisher et al. 2018). Inferred: the presence/exchange split and the averaging. Open: averaging vs. taking the minimum.

## Sources
Petrov (2006), ICCM pp. 391–392\[1\] [PR];\[1\]\[2\] Fisher, Houpt & Gunzelmann (2018), *Computational Brain & Behavior*, doi:10.1007/s42113-018-0015-3 [PR];\[3\] Anderson & Schooler (1991) [PR].

# R-05 Importance
*File: research/findings/R-05-importance.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
Generative Agents has a model rate importance 1–10 at write time; MonoAgens cannot call a model in the gate. Instead:

importance ∈ [0, 1] = max(i_reason, i_commit, i_surprise)
- i_reason: supplied by reasoning when it writes the memory; otherwise 0.
- i_commit: 1 if the memory is linked to a promise, goal or expectation.
- i_surprise: clamp(z/z_max, 0, 1), where z is the prediction-error z-score at encoding (R-23).

## Parameters

|---|---|---|---|---|
| z_max | z mapped to 1 | 3 | 2–5 | tune in Lab |

## Neutral value
importance_weight = 0.

## Claim
Without it, 30-day recall of commitment-linked and surprising items falls to that of ordinary items.

## How the Lab would test it
Bury promises and surprising receipts among routine events; probe recall at 7 and 30 days.

## Established / inferred / open
Established: importance as a retrieval term (Park et al. 2023); better memory at event boundaries (Zacks et al. 2007). Inferred: the max. Open: how well i_reason is calibrated.

# R-07 Goals, chains and threads
*File: research/findings/R-07-goals-threads.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
**Definitions.**
- **Chain:** the events linked by `caused_by` back to an origin; `origin_actor` is inherited along it.
- **Goal:** an expectation of kind `goal`, created only by reasoning via `expect`. It holds the chain, places, people, a completion condition (description and vector), a due time and an expiry. It ends when it is resolved, answered, expired or revised away. The lifecycle is in code.
- **Task thread:** the events attributed to one active goal. **Main thread:** everything else.

**Attribution of an incoming event, in order:**
1. A receipt or reply-link to the goal's chain: attributed, and the breaker fires.
2. A match to the goal's open prediction: attributed, and the breaker fires.
3. Cosine to the condition ≥ wake_gate_lo, plus one classifier call: attributed, no breaker; relevance is raised.
4. Otherwise: main thread.

## Parameters
| Name | Meaning | Start value | Range | Source or "tune in Lab" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| default goal expiry | | 24 h | 1 h–30 d | tune in Lab |

## Neutral value
The goal clause is removed from the breaker.

## Claim
Without it, multi-step tasks stall when receipts arrive in busy places.

## How the Lab would test it
Send receipts amid group chatter. Run with the goal clause on and off, and with step 3 allowed or not allowed to fire the breaker.

## Established / inferred / open
Established:
- Goals bias processing (Miller & Cohen 2001).
- A typed intention store took Gemma-E2B from 4.2% to 66.2% Set-F1 on PM-Bench.
- Pro-Memory updates intentions only on actions actually issued or on evidence of resolution.

Open: nested goals.

## Sources
Miller & Cohen (2001) [PR]; Zhao & Wu (2026), arXiv 2609.01272 [PP];\[5\] Bei et al. (2026), arXiv 2611.05731 [PP].\[6\]

# R-08 Timing by medium
*File: research/findings/R-08-timing.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
Media differ in eight grounding constraints, and without cotemporality, timing cannot serve as a cue (Clark & Brennan 1991). So durations are learned per place, seeded by a typical latency the body declares:
- patience(P) = the q_p quantile of the latency to the first third-party reply;
- expiry(P) = the q_e quantile of the latency from an act to the addressee's next event, times a stakes factor;
- the silence window (§20) uses the same rule for anyone's reply.

Each estimate is shrunk toward the prior with n₀ pseudo-observations. Answering evidence is a `done` receipt, the addressee's next event in the place, or a continued-attention number reported by the body.

## Parameters
| Name | Meaning | Start value | Range | Source or "tune in Lab" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| q_p, q_e | quantiles | 0.5, 0.9 | 0.3–0.8; 0.8–0.99 | tune in Lab |
| n₀ | prior weight | 10 | 3–50 | tune in Lab |
| spoken prior gap | | 0.2 s | language means ±0.25 s | Stivers et al. 2009 |

## Neutral value
Body prior, never updated.

## Claim
Without it, false "no reply" verdicts rise in slow places, and missed absences rise in fast ones.

## How the Lab would test it
Run one scenario at seconds-scale and at hours-scale latency, with learned vs. fixed timing.

## Established / inferred / open
Established: Clark & Brennan (1991). Across 10 languages, gaps cluster at 0–200 ms; Danish has the longest mean gap (+469 ms) and Japanese the shortest (+7 ms) (Stivers et al. 2009). Open: how purpose changes the evidence required.

## Sources
Clark & Brennan (1991) [PR]; Stivers et al. (2009), *PNAS* 106(26), doi:10.1073/pnas.0903616106 [PR].\[7\]

# R-09 Sense of agency
*File: research/findings/R-09-agency.md*

**Status:** answered (rule); numbers open

## Answer
Predictions get a kind:
- *effect*: what the soul's act should change (a comparator prediction);
- *response*: what someone else should do next (a grounding prediction).

Only effect matches are self-caused. An event matches when:
1. **Echo:** the gateway maps the actor to the soul's id, or the event carries the act's correlation id; or
2. **Physical:** the event is on the predicted channel, within [t_x + lat_min, t_x + lat_max], and |Δ_obs − Δ_pred| ≤ k·σ_channel.

Anything else is external, and an unmet effect prediction becomes an error. Self-caused events get basis `self` and are not appraised.

## Parameters
| Name | Meaning | Start value | Range | Source or "tune in Lab" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lat_min, lat_max | effect window | body-declared | — | capability fact |
| k | tolerance | 2σ | 1–4 | tune in Lab |

## Neutral value
No self-caused marking.

## Claim
Without it, the soul reacts to its own echoes (wakes per 100 acts).

## How the Lab would test it
An echoing chat body and a door-sensor simulator; count self-reactions and false "self" labels.

## Established / inferred / open
Established: the comparator model. Inferred: the two kinds and the matching rules. Open: values per body.

## Sources
Frith, Blakemore & Wolpert (2000), *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* [PR].

# R-10 A promise that cannot be kept on time
*File: research/findings/R-10-promise-failure.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
What the literature supports:
- Completed intentions are deactivated gradually, "not like a light switch" (Möschl et al. 2020).
- Suspended intentions stay active, while finished ones are quickly deactivated.
- Delaying execution after retrieval is fragile: brief interruptions significantly lower execution (McDaniel et al. 2004).
- PIS keeps the lifecycle in code; Pro-Memory updates intentions only on actions issued or evidence of resolution.

Rule:
- States: pending → due → attempted → kept | failed → retry | overdue | renegotiated | cancelled.
- A failed, refused, held or unknown receipt is not completion. The intention stays suspended, still counts in promise_debt, and is retried at t + b·2^(i−1) for attempts i ≤ n_max.
- After n_max failures, or when a retry cannot finish in time, the breaker wakes reasoning to inform the person or renegotiate.
- At start-up, promises with due < now are marked overdue (promise_debt = 1), and reasoning wakes once per place. "Kept late" is recorded separately from "kept".

## Parameters
| Name | Meaning | Start value | Range | Source or "tune in Lab" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| n_max | retries | 3 | 1–5 | tune in Lab |
| b | first backoff | 1 min | 10 s–1 h | tune in Lab |

## Neutral value
No retry and no overdue handling.

## Claim
Without it, kept + kept-late falls under failures and downtime.

## How the Lab would test it
A PM-Bench-style week with `failed` receipts and a 6-hour outage.

## Established / inferred / open
Inferred: the state machine and the backoff. Open: retry counts, and when renegotiating beats retrying.

## Sources
Möschl et al. (2020), *Psychological Bulletin* (PMC7007322) [PR];\[8\] McDaniel et al. (2004), *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 18 [PR]; arXiv 2609.01272, 2611.05731, 2607.12385 [PP].

# R-11 Context without a transcript
*File: research/findings/R-11-context-rebuild.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
The evidence favours a rebuilt context, as long as the agent's own reasoning and actions stay verbatim.
- On SWE-bench Verified, Lindenbauer et al. (JetBrains Research / TU Munich, arXiv 2508.21433) found that "both Observation Masking and LLM-Summary more than halve the cost compared to the Raw Agent", without significantly lowering the solve rate. With Qwen3-Coder 480B, for example, masking cut cost by 52.7% ($1.29 → $0.61) at a 54.8% vs. 53.4% solve rate.
- Summarization did not consistently beat masking.
- Verbatim memory beats lossy extraction (An 2026).

Context order, from stable to volatile:
1. identity;
2. cards of the people present, and the self-card;
3. commitments and the active goal;
4. the soul's own thoughts and acts verbatim, the last M observations verbatim, older ones as id placeholders;
5. the top-k recalled memories, with basis and time;
6. the last N events of the place;
7. `<now>` and the trigger.

## Parameters
| Name | Meaning | Start value | Range | Source or "tune in Lab" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M | verbatim observations | 10 | 3–20 | Lindenbauer et al. (M = 10 was their best window); tune in Lab |
| N | place events | 20 | 5–60 | tune in Lab |
| k | recalled memories | 5 | 3–15 | tune in Lab |

## Neutral value
The full transcript.

## Claim
The rebuilt context is non-inferior on success, and better on cost and on recovery after a restart.

## How the Lab would test it
Transcript vs. rebuild, plus a restart in the middle of a task. Measure success, tokens and cache hits, with a non-inferiority margin fixed in advance.

## Established / inferred / open
Open: no study compares a rebuilt context with a transcript on long multi-party tasks; the Lab has to produce that result.

## Sources
Lindenbauer et al. (2025), arXiv 2508.21433 [PP]; An (2026), arXiv 2601.00821 [PP].

- revise(target_id, new_version), which may supersede memories, belief statements, expectation conditions and times, and self-card claims. It may **not** change log events, identity, credence counts or dispositions.

**Where recall belongs (three options):**
- A. A sixth act. Simplest for reasoning, but it breaks "five acts".
- B. A capability invoked through act. It keeps five acts and matches ARCHITECTURE.md ("recall the whole log … leaves through the gateway's doors"). It costs one loop turn and lets the valve filter private items.
- C. A field of think. No new act, but recall is not recorded as its own decision.

Recommended: B, the only option that needs no change to the invariants.

# R-14 Backlog chunking
*File: research/findings/R-14-backlog.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
An event is backlog if its ingest lag exceeds patience(P), or if it arrived while the soul was down or asleep. A place's backlog is appraised as one chunk:

s_k = max_i s_k(e_i)·(1 + L_i)^(−d)

The max keeps volume alone from firing the gate. Any commitment in the chunk fires the breaker. At most four place-chunks are handled per wake, in order of salience.

## Parameters

## Neutral value
Each event appraised on its own.

## How the Lab would test it
An outage with 200 messages across 5 places.

# R-15 Control of report
*File: research/findings/R-15-report-control.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
From §14's utility, the soul should volunteer an answer when c·v > (1−c)·k. So:

P_rc = max(certainty_bar, k/(v+k))

The criterion rises with the cost of an error relative to the value of being right, which is the payoff effect Koriat & Goldsmith (1996) showed. If c < P_rc: try up to n_recall recalls, then a coarser grain, then withhold. A coarser grain means: exact time → day → period; name → "someone in the group"; number → range.

# R-17 Source monitoring without reading content
*File: research/findings/R-17-source-monitoring.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
The question's premise conflates events and propositions. The event "X said P" is *observed*; P itself is *told* by X. So basis belongs to beliefs and memories, not to events:
- observed: about logged events the soul witnessed (utterances, receipts, sensor readings);

Reasoning assigns the basis. The core then checks it: an `observed` label is downgraded to `inferred` if its links do not reach percepts the soul witnessed. Across bodies, sensor readings are observed and another agent's message is told.

# R-34 Hard forgetting
*File: research/findings/R-34-forgetting.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
Deleted content survives in indices, summaries and other derived artifacts, and comes back through retrieval ("backflow"). The remedy is dependency-aware deletion with reference counting (arXiv 2602.17692). For hard forgetting:
1. Remove the events and their vectors.
2. Remove gists, lessons and procedures whose sources are all forgotten; regenerate the ones that still have other sources.
3. Remove credence evidence that came from those events.
4. Remove the related ledger and index entries.
5. Write a forgetting event that names ids only.
6. Snapshot the derived state at that event, so later replay starts from there.

## Parameters
None.
## Neutral value
Not a mechanism.
## Claim
Without dependency-aware deletion, forgotten facts resurface.
## How the Lab would test it
Forget a person's messages, then probe every recall and gist path.
## Established / inferred / open
Open: whether credences must be recomputed.
## Sources
arXiv 2602.17692 [PP]; Ding et al. (2026), arXiv 2606.30306 [PP].\[29\]

# R-39 Baselines
*File: research/findings/R-39-baselines.md*

**Status:** partly answered

## Answer
**Hermes Agent** (Nous Research, MIT licence):
- keeps agent-curated memory files that are injected into the system prompt at session start;
- searches past sessions with FTS5 and LLM summaries;
- writes its own skills.

**Memory poisoning.** Dash et al. ("From Untrusted Input to Trusted Memory", AIWILD@ICML 2026, arXiv 2606.04329) tested 3,240 attack cases with GPT-OSS-120B. They report: "Average ASR is 66.67% on HERMES compared to 34.25% on OpenClaw, and average RSR is 64.70% compared to 17.40%". They attribute this to Hermes's permissive write policy and its frozen-snapshot injection.

**Gaps.** Hermes's documentation shows no cheap gate and no model of when to speak in groups.

**Shared benchmarks:** PM-Bench, STALE, LoCoMo, LongMemEval, GroupMemBench (arXiv 2605.14498) and PITCH.

## How the Lab would test it
Run the same scenarios through a Hermes adapter and through MonoAgens, with the same metrics and cost.

## Sources
Hermes Agent documentation [IND]; arXiv 2606.04329 [PP]; arXiv 2605.14498 [PP].
